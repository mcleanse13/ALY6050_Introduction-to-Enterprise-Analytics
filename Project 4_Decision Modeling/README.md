This project focuses on designing a prescriptive inventory management model for a company that sells key engine components, with the objective of balancing sufficient inventory to meet demand while minimizing total costs. Using historical data on annual demand, unit costs, holding costs, and ordering costs, the project applies decision modeling techniques to identify the optimal order quantity that minimizes total inventory cost. Analyses include calculating annual holding and ordering costs, constructing data tables and scatterplots to visualize cost behaviors, and applying Excel Solver and R functions to verify the most cost-effective order quantity. A what-if analysis is also conducted to assess how changes in model parameters, such as holding cost rates or demand levels, impact total costs, providing insight into the sensitivity and robustness of the inventory strategy.

The second part of the project introduces uncertainty through simulation, employing triangular, uniform, normal, and exponential distributions to model variations in demand and order intervals. A total of 1,000 simulated occurrences estimate the expected minimum total cost, expected order quantity, and expected annual number of orders, with confidence intervals calculated for each metric. These simulations validate the robustness of the decision model under varying conditions, offering the organization a data-driven framework to make strategic inventory decisions. Overall, the project demonstrates how prescriptive modeling and simulation tools can optimize inventory management, maximize profitability, and prepare the company to adapt to dynamic market conditions.
